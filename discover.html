How this started

A week that didn’t end
when people flew home.

In October 2025, eight people came to Puerto Escondido. All of them sitting with sacred plant medicine for the first time. Seven days, held inside one container.

Six months later, they still talk every day. They call each other familia, in their own words. One of them, Steve, said seven months out he still thinks about it most days. Olga called it one of the most meaningful experiences of her life. Anna called it her highlight of the year.

That retreat proved something. Not that plant medicine is powerful. That part was already known. It proved that the right container, held by the right people, can turn a week into something that doesn’t end when people fly home.

November 2026 is the next chapter of that.

Who’s holding this

Containers are only as strong
as the people holding them.

Humberto Alcalá
Shaman & Ceremony Facilitator

A decade in ceremony, trained in the Shipibo tradition of Peru, with over 500 ceremonies behind him. What sets him apart is how he bridges the modern world and the ceremony itself. The work feels grounded and relatable, never distant. He lives what he teaches.

Aleck Urtaza
Mindfulness Facilitator & Integration Lead

Sound therapist, musician, and breathwork facilitator, keeper of ancestral instruments. He weaves sound and breath into the container and holds the field before, during, and long after ceremony. The grounding presence through the week.

Allie Schlener
Energetic Support & Intuitive Guide

Over ten years in holistic wellness, energy work, Reiki, and intuitive healing. She holds space for deep remembrance, helping people reconnect to their inner wisdom. When she is in the container, the field deepens and the feminine is present.

Trevor Johnston
Founder & Curator

Curates the whole experience. Not the facilitator, but the vision-holder and container-builder, a participant in ceremony alongside everyone else. He is in the room for the opening circle, the closing circle, and everything in between.

Every participant has a real conversation with the team before being accepted. This isn’t a transaction. It’s a container, and containers are only as strong as the people holding them.

What this actually is

A community rooted in
remembrance and presence.

We create spaces where people slow down, listen deeply, and reconnect with themselves through lived experience. We are not here to heal or fix anyone. We are here to walk beside one another.

The retreats are intentionally small. Ten to fifteen people. Seven days. A structure that moves from arrival, to ceremony, to integration, with nothing rushed and nothing wasted.

But the retreat is only one part of it. In Puerto Escondido, a community gathers year round. Music and Mantras at sunset. Wellness Saturdays. Breathwork and sound journeys. Over 200 people who have been gathering for more than a year.

The retreat is the deepest expression of something already alive.

The next chapter

November 23–30, 2026.

Seven days in Puerto Escondido, at a private oceanview villa held exclusively for the group. Three sacred medicines woven through the week: Ayahuasca, held overnight by Humberto and his team. Satori, his own proprietary blend, gentler but equally clarifying. Bufo, brief and complete, closing the sequence. Kambo offered as an optional physical purge before the deepest ceremony.

Nothing is mandatory. Every ceremony is a choice made in the moment, and the container holds you either way.

The week isn’t only ceremony. Two days to arrive and settle. An excursion day for hot springs or the beach. A closing Thanksgiving dinner together before everyone leaves. Three months of integration support after you return home, because the work doesn’t end when the retreat does.

Where this is

Puerto Escondido,
on the coast of Oaxaca.

Puerto Escondido is a coastal town in Oaxaca, on the southern Pacific side of Mexico. The retreat is held in La Barra, a quieter corner of the coast, five minutes on foot to a pristine beach and ten minutes from La Punta and Zicatela.

The nearest airport is Puerto Escondido International (PXM). Direct flights are available from Mexico City (MEX) and Oaxaca City (OAX). Most international travelers fly into Mexico City first, then connect to Puerto Escondido. From the airport, the villa is about fifteen minutes by taxi.

In late November, Puerto Escondido sits in the mid to high 20s Celsius and the ocean is warm. If you’ve never been, that’s part of what people fall for. You don’t need to have any of the logistics figured out before you reach out.
