What this is

Seven days.
One chapter closing.
Another beginning.

This is not a wellness weekend. It is not a yoga retreat with an optional sound bath. It is a structured, intentional container — seven days in Puerto Escondido, held by a team who has done this work themselves, guided by a shaman with nearly a decade of ceremony across four continents.

The first two days are for arriving. For slowing down enough to remember why you came. The ceremonies come later, once the group has landed, connected, and set intention together. The final days are for integrating — holding what came up, letting the realizations settle, and preparing to carry it home.

Everything in between is designed to support that arc. The meals, the movement, the excursions, the quiet time. None of it is filler. All of it is the container.

The medicines

Four medicines.
One sequence.

Each medicine has been chosen with intention. The combination is specific, potent, and complementary. This is a genuine introduction to sacred medicine, held in a container that is structured, safe, and beginner friendly. The majority of our October 2025 participants had no prior experience. All ceremonies are held by Humberto Alcalá and his team.

01
Ayahuasca
The vine of remembrance
Night ceremony

Brewed from two Amazonian plants with deep roots in indigenous healing traditions, Ayahuasca works like a mirror held to the interior of your life. It surfaces what has been buried, clarifies what has been obscured, and creates conditions for emotional release and deep insight.

The ceremony is long, often demanding, and facilitated by Humberto within a traditional Shipibo framework, with live music throughout from Aleck and the ceremonial team. What comes up is specific to each person. The container holds whatever arrives.

02
Kambo
The purifier
Morning · optional

Kambo is not a psychedelic. It is a powerful physical purge derived from the secretion of the Amazonian tree frog. Intense, brief, and deeply cleansing. It is used before the Ayahuasca ceremony because of what it does to the body — it clears, prepares, and opens.

The experience lasts thirty to forty minutes. Most participants describe feeling completely different on the other side of it. Lighter. More present. Ready. Kambo is optional and held with care and intention.

03
Satori
The medicine of clarity
Afternoon ceremony

Satori is unique to Humberto. A proprietary blend drawn from two plants with roots in Mexican and Middle Eastern healing traditions. Taken in capsule form. Gentler on the body than Ayahuasca but equally powerful in what it produces — clarity, emotional release, and a quality of insight that tends to arrive quietly and stay.

Participants often describe Satori as the medicine that makes sense of everything that came before it in the sequence. It is its own teacher, with its own rhythm and energetic signature.

04
Bufo
The dissolution
Morning · outdoors

Bufo is brief. Fifteen to twenty minutes. It is not a visionary journey. It is a complete reset of the sense of self, held in the open air, facilitated one person at a time. Many describe it as the most significant experience of their lives. Others describe it as returning home to a place they did not know they had forgotten.

It is not frightening if you are prepared for it. The preparation happens in the days prior. Bufo is the final medicine in the sequence for a reason. It closes what was opened.

We honor these medicines as teachers. They are not taken recreationally. They are not shortcuts. Each one is served from a place of reverence and held within a container designed to support what they reveal.

La Barra beach is five minutes on foot. Several days include ocean time. Sunset gatherings and informal rituals on the sand.
Hot springs
A day trip to natural hot springs in the surrounding mountains. A natural way to decompress between ceremony nights.
Intentional downtime
No agenda. No schedule. The pool, the hammocks, the rooftop, the group. Just being — with nowhere to be. This is part of the design.

Excursions are offered as a group where possible. Some days you may prefer to stay at the villa. The pool, hammocks, and rooftop are always available.

Common questions

Answered honestly.

Do I need prior experience with plant medicine?+
No. All eight participants at the October 2025 retreat were first-timers. The container is specifically designed to be a genuine, safe introduction — not an experience that assumes prior knowledge. Humberto has worked with first-time participants in the vast majority of his 500+ ceremonies. What matters is readiness, not experience.
Can I come alone? Will I know anyone?+
Most people arrive alone. By the end of day two, that changes. The preparation days are specifically designed to build genuine connection within the group before any ceremony begins. The people you go through this with tend to become some of the most significant relationships of your life — not because of sentiment, but because of what you witness together.
What if I have a difficult experience in ceremony?+
Difficult experiences are part of the work. They are not failures and they are not emergencies. Humberto, Aleck, and Allie are present throughout every ceremony — not as observers, but as active holders of the space. If something intense arises, someone is always with you. The pre-retreat conversation also prepares you for what a challenging moment might look and feel like, and what to do when it arrives: surrender rather than resist.
I take medication. Can I still participate?+
This depends entirely on the medication. SSRIs, MAOIs, antidepressants, blood thinners, and certain heart medications have known interactions with these medicines and in some cases represent genuine contraindications. This conversation happens in your pre-retreat screening — before you commit — so that Trevor and the team can assess fit honestly. We will never encourage someone to participate who shouldn't.
What is the dietary preparation?+
You will receive a full dietary guide after your spot is confirmed. Generally: reduce or eliminate alcohol, recreational drugs, processed foods, pork, and sexual activity in the week before arrival. Avoid SSRIs and MAOIs for a minimum of two weeks prior — consult your doctor. The preparation is not punishing. The cleaner the body arrives, the more available it is for the work.
Do I have to do all of the ceremonies?+
No. Every ceremony is a choice made in the moment. You can participate in all of them, some of them, or one of them. Choosing not to participate in a ceremony on a given day does not remove you from the container — you remain part of the group and are held by the team throughout. The container does not pressure anyone toward any medicine. It only invites.
What happens after the retreat?+
Three months of integration support through the Sacred Journeys community. The October 2025 group still communicates every day — seven months later, Steve described still thinking about the experience most days. The ceremony reveals. Integration is where transformation actually lands. The team takes the post-retreat period seriously because this is where the work either stabilizes or dissipates. The container is designed to make sure it stabilizes.
How do I confirm my spot?+
If you've received this page, you've already had the first conversation. The next step is confirming which room tier works for you and placing a 50% deposit. Spots are limited to 10–15 people and filled by conversation, not first-come-first-served. The right fit matters more than the timeline.
